Kleer

Taking responsibility for a mobile product, from the first customer conversation to the App Store and Google Play.

Everyday admin on the go

Kleer connects time reporting, expenses, payroll and accounting. In the mobile app, employees can log time and photograph receipts on the spot. Managers can approve reports in the app, and the approved information feeds into payroll and accounting. Receipts and hours can move through the same workflow without being entered again.

See the Kleer app

From outsourced app to in-house ownership

I joined to help bring mobile development in-house and rebuild the app in Flutter. I worked with specialist consultants early on to bring the knowledge into the team, and went on to work as the sole developer on the mobile app. I handled planning, prioritisation, design, architecture, development, testing and releases. I also ran product demos, met customers and followed up on their feedback.

Customer conversations shape the next release

In a demo I want to see how someone would use the product in their own work. With an expense, for example, I want to understand what the employee needs to get the receipt submitted on the spot and what the manager needs to approve it later. That gives me something concrete to discuss with the customer and helps me decide what to improve first. As a product engineer I still lead mobile development and now also work on AI-assisted development, automation and making the team’s tools easier to use.

Widas Mattekasse

A weekly kit of maths games for children and parents to play together.

It started with Wida

My wife Wida grew up in Indonesia and trained there as a maths teacher. When she began working in a Swedish preschool, she missed some of what had made teaching meaningful to her in Indonesia. In her own experience, she sometimes felt closer to a babysitter than a teacher. She considered starting a library book club or a programme like the after-school activities she knew from Indonesia. She wanted somewhere to bring play and learning together.

Those conversations gave me the idea for Widas Mattekasse. It brings together her experience as a maths teacher and her interest in early childhood education. I was also worried that children in Sweden could lose opportunities to build maths skills and practise critical thinking as they grow up with AI and instant answers. I wanted to give families more chances to practise both in everyday life.

Recipes for time together

I borrowed the idea of a weekly kit from Linas Matkasse. The app recommends a handful of games based on the child’s age and interests and what fits the family’s week. Each recipe gives the parent simple materials, steps and questions to ask. With a few mandarins and three bowls, for example, you can explore what it means to share equally. Then you add another mandarin and ask what happens. The parent can put the phone away and feel confident leading a creative game with their child.

Built with
Flutter and Firebase.
Read more and try Widas Mattekasse

A workshop for Wida

I built an admin area where Wida can write her own games or bring in AI-generated drafts to edit and review. She decides what is ready to publish. A dashboard compares demand with the games already available by age, interests and where families want to play. It helps her see which kinds of games are running short and choose what to create next.

Slotcars

From experimenting with map data to racing slot cars on real streets.

Playing with real streets

Slotcars grew out of my interest in Formula 1 and an earlier app idea that also used map data. I started playing with map data, trying to turn a few junctions into a loop you could drive. Eventually it became a game where you draw a circuit along real streets, then race with up to four people on the same screen. I still find it ridiculously fun.

Built with
Flutter, OpenStreetMap data, Cloudflare Pages for downloadable maps, and Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics.
Read more and try Slotcars

A smooth web game with zero hosting cost

I wanted to see how far I could push Flutter on the web. A race has to respond the instant you lift your thumb to brake, so I kept the controls simple and adapted camera options to what worked well in the browser. Players can share a challenge as a link across iOS, Android and the web. I kept the initial download smaller by packaging extra city maps as on-demand downloads. Races run on the device and the maps are static files, so I can operate the game without paying for hosting.

Patchlands & Patchwise

One puzzle, two ways to feel at home in it.

I wanted both a playful version and a quiet, minimalist version of the same puzzle. I built two apps on a shared game engine, which let me make different visual choices while keeping the rules and puzzle behaviour consistent.

Patchlands

How do I make an abstract puzzle inviting? I turned the board into a vegetable garden. The crops give the irregular pieces distinct identities, and a solo campaign gives players a place to learn the rules before trying multiplayer.

Built with
Flutter, a local database for offline play, and Firebase for online games.
Read more and try Patchlands

Patchwise

How much can I strip back while keeping the puzzle clear? I used plain wooden pieces, a muted palette and a dark mode. That puts the focus on the shape of each piece and the space you have left.

Built with
Flutter and the shared Patchlands game engine.

Promo Pals Bali

A friend’s spreadsheet became a community guide to eating out in Bali.

From Excel to making plans

I spent a lot of time in Bali while my wife lived there. Coming from Sweden, I was surprised by how many restaurants and bars ran specials on particular days of the week. A friend had started keeping track of them in Excel. I turned that list into an app so I could see what was on each day and plan where to eat or grab a drink. My friends found it useful too.

Keeping deals current together

I let anyone suggest a deal or report one that had changed or expired. That gave the community a way to help keep the listings current without relying on me to check every offer. I wrote the app by hand before Claude Code existed. I haven’t worked on it in years, and people still use it.

Built with
Flutter and Firebase.

Nutrimons

A mobile game that turns grocery barcodes into monsters shaped by the food’s nutrition.

From barcode to monster team

Each scan looks up the product and its nutrition through the Open Food Facts API. The food’s nutrients help shape the monster’s stats, while its category helps set its type. The same barcode always leads to the same creature, tying the collection to real products you discover.

Catch monsters for your dex, raise a team and scan a friend’s team QR code to start a duel.

Built with
Flutter, Firebase, Google ML Kit for Android barcode scanning, Apple Vision for iOS scanning, the Open Food Facts API, and native AR via ARCore (Android) and ARKit (iOS).
See Nutrimons on the workbench

Fighting in the camera view

On supported phones, ARCore or ARKit tracks the space around a package and anchors the monster beside it. Move the phone or turn away and the creature stays where it appeared. Choose Fight and an animated battle plays over the live camera view.

Barcode recognition runs on the device. The native AR scanner shares its camera frames with barcode scanning, then keeps the monster in place with a world anchor.
