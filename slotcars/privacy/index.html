Slotcars · privacy policy

Race real streets.
Your circuits stay yours.

Slotcars lets you trace a loop through real city streets and race toy slot cars around it, with up to four drivers on one screen. Everything you make stays on your device. The game sends Google anonymous counts of what gets played, and reports of crashes, so we can see what to improve.

Effective November 22, 2026 Operator Kodsnickerboa · Sweden
Short version

Slotcars has no accounts, ads, online play, cloud saves or payments, and it works without an internet connection. Your circuits, lap records and race settings are saved on your device and nowhere else. The app uses Google's Firebase services to count how the game is used, measure its speed, report crashes and, if you allow it, send notifications. None of that includes your name, your circuits or an advertising ID.

This policy describes Slotcars for Android and iOS and the version played in a web browser at kodsnickerboa.com/slotcars. Earlier versions of the app (1.0.x) sent nothing at all. It will be updated if the app adds accounts, online play, payments, advertising, or another data-collecting service.

1. Scope

This privacy policy explains how Kodsnickerboa handles information in Slotcars for Android and iOS, and in the version played in a web browser at kodsnickerboa.com/slotcars. Slotcars is a racing game for one to four drivers sharing one phone or tablet: you trace a circuit on a city map built into the app, then race on it, alone against the clock or against each other.

The policy applies to the app and not to the independent privacy practices of Apple, Google, or the device and operating-system providers you use. Google is named below where the app itself sends information to it.

2. Data we process

Slotcars never asks for an account, a name, an email address, your contacts, your location, the camera, the microphone or your photos. The only permission it can ask for is to send notifications, and only when you tap "Tell me about new cities". The information it handles falls into two groups:

Stored locally on your device

The circuits you trace (name, city and route), the fastest lap and sector times on each circuit with the date they were set, and the race settings you used last, such as the number of drivers and laps, item strips and whether sound is on. This never leaves your device.

Sent to Google Firebase

Counts of what is played, how fast the app runs, crash reports, and a notification address if you allow notifications. Each comes with a random ID for this installation of the app and general device information. The details are below.

Usage counts (Google Analytics for Firebase)

The app records that a race was started, finished or left, that a circuit was saved, that a best lap was beaten, and whether notifications were allowed. With a race it sends the city, the number of drivers and laps, the race format, whether items, a following camera or a night race were chosen, how long the race took and how often cars came off the track. With a saved circuit it sends the city and whether you traced it or used "Surprise me". Circuit names, routes and lap times are not sent. Google Analytics adds general information by itself: first opens, sessions and time in the app, device model, operating-system and app version, language, and the country or region worked out from your IP address. The app is built without the advertising ID (IDFA on iOS, AAID on Android) and does not collect it.

Speed measurements (Firebase Performance Monitoring)

How long the app takes to start, to load a city's map and to prepare a race, together with device model, operating-system and app version, country and network type.

Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics) · Android and iOS

When the app crashes or hits an error it did not expect, it sends a report: where in the code it happened, the device model, operating-system and app version, free memory and disk space, the time, and a random ID for this installation. With it come the usage counts recorded just before the error.

Remote settings (Firebase Remote Config)

At startup the app asks Firebase for a few settings we can change without an update, such as which cities are offered. The request carries the random installation ID, the app version, language and country.

Notifications and messages (Firebase Cloud Messaging and In-App Messaging) · Android and iOS

Only if you tap "Tell me about new cities" and allow notifications does the app register a notification address (a push token) with Firebase and, on iOS, Apple. We use it to send occasional news about the game to everyone who allowed it, not to you individually. The app can also show a message inside the game, chosen by Firebase from the usage counts and the installation ID.

In a web browser

The web version asks before it counts anything: Google Analytics, which stores cookies in your browser, stays off unless you answer yes to the question on the home screen. Remote settings and speed measurements run without asking and keep a random installation ID in the browser's storage. The web version has no crash reports, notifications or in-app messages.

The maps of London, Stockholm, Paris, Rome, Sydney and New York are built from OpenStreetMap data, © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database License. They are bundled with the app, so viewing or racing on a map does not contact OpenStreetMap or anyone else.

3. How we use information

We use information only for the following purposes:

  • to run Slotcars and keep your circuits, lap records and race settings available on your device;
  • to show your best laps and sector times, and to let your best lap drive beside you as a ghost, when you race a circuit again;
  • to learn which cities, race formats and features are used and where players give up, so we know what to improve;
  • to find and fix crashes and slow spots;
  • to switch features or cities on and off without an app update;
  • to tell you about new cities and other news, if you allowed notifications.

Where the GDPR applies, we rely on our legitimate interest in keeping the game working and knowing how it is used for usage counts, speed measurements, crash reports and remote settings in the Android and iOS apps, and on your consent for notifications and for Google Analytics in the web version.

Slotcars does not contain an advertising SDK, does not track you across apps or websites, and does not sell personal information.

4. Who receives information

The information described in section 2 as sent to Google Firebase is processed for us by Google (Google Ireland Limited in the EEA, Google LLC elsewhere), under Google's data-processing terms. Google may process it in the United States and other countries; transfers from the EEA rely on the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework and the European Commission's standard contractual clauses. Google's own description is in the Firebase privacy information.

We do not sell information, do not share it with advertisers, and do not give it to anyone else. Nothing you create in the game is shared with anyone.

Apple and Google may separately process information as part of their app-store, device, and operating-system services, under their own policies.

5. Choices, retention, and deletion

Local data

Data stored by Slotcars stays on your device until you delete it. To delete a circuit together with its lap records, tap the bin on its card on the home screen, or press and hold the card. Uninstalling the app or clearing its data in your device settings removes everything. Device backups made by iOS or Android may contain a copy, under your backup settings.

Information sent to Google Firebase

Usage counts are kept for up to 14 months, crash reports and speed measurements for 90 days, after which Google deletes them. The Android and iOS apps have no switch for usage counts, speed measurements or crash reports. Uninstalling the app stops all of it, and a new installation gets a new random ID that is not linked to the old one. You can turn notifications off at any time in your device's settings. In the web version, clearing this site's data in your browser removes the cookies and the installation ID, and the question about usage counts is asked again.

To object to this processing, or to ask for the information tied to your installation to be deleted, write to the address in section 8.

6. Your rights

Depending on where you live, applicable privacy law may give you rights to request access to, correction of, deletion of, or restriction of personal data, and to object to or limit certain processing. You may also have a right to data portability or to withdraw consent where processing is based on consent.

To exercise a right, contact Kodsnickerboa. We may need to ask for information needed to understand and verify the request, and we will respond within the time required by applicable law. You may also complain to the data-protection authority in the country where you live or work. In Sweden, this is Integritetsskyddsmyndigheten (IMY).

7. Children

Slotcars does not knowingly request names, email addresses, or other directly identifying information from children, shows no advertising, and does not collect an advertising ID. If you are a parent or guardian and believe that a child has provided us with personal information, please contact us so we can review the request and take appropriate action.

8. Contact and changes

The privacy point of contact is Kodsnickerboa, the name used by an individual developer in Sweden who makes apps for fun. For privacy questions or requests, write privately to hello.slotcars@kodsnickerboa.com.

We may update this policy when Slotcars' data practices change or when required by law. The effective date at the top of this page identifies the latest version.
